This manuscript extends our SECRYPT 2026 paper, "On Automating the Obfuscation of Malware Payloads: Zone, a bin2bin Metamorphic Engine" (DOI: 10.5220/0015170600004103). The conference paper introduced the Zone architecture and the Position-Independent Instruction design, evaluated native transformations on 102 Windows x64 and x86 payloads, and compared their functional and static-detection outcomes with those of Shikata Ga Nai. The extended manuscript cites this study and identifies reused figures, tables, listings, and the algorithm through source attribution. These earlier methods and results provide the baseline for the additional research; the title, abstract, and conclusions have been rewritten to reflect the extended scope.

The extension adds a code-data preparation layer with alternative symbolic/heuristic and LLM-based identification, shared handling of preserved data and supported references, an LLM transformation path for four transformation families, and integration of the existing assembly-wrapping technique on both architectures. The new evaluation includes independently annotated classification programs, controlled functional programs, a comparison of data identification on 19 original payloads, and completed wrapping and LLM transformation campaigns covering the full 102-input corpus. It also examines recovery on 49 previously problematic inputs and presents an overall working-rate update that explicitly combines new and historical evidence. The analysis separates construction success, functional outcomes, static detection, and recorded resource use, and compares zero-shot and two-shot prompting. These additions supply new methods, implementation contributions, experiments, and discussion beyond the conference study.
