This manuscript is an extended and revised version of our SECRYPT 2026 paper, "On Automating the Obfuscation of Malware Payloads: Zone, a bin2bin Metamorphic Engine" (DOI: 10.5220/0015170600004103). The conference paper introduced the Zone architecture and Position-Independent Instruction design and evaluated native transformations on 102 Windows payloads. The extension explicitly identifies its relationship to that work in the introduction and in the comparison with related research. It cites all reused or adapted objects, rewrites the title, abstract, conclusions, and explanatory prose, and compresses the historical presentation. The historical detection plot is identified as a reanalysis of retained scan records with corrected configuration labels.

The new research adds code-data identification and shared data handling, separate LLM classification and transformation paths, and integration and evaluation of assembly wrapping. New experiments include six annotated classification programs, 13 controlled functional programs, data-handling comparisons on 19 original payloads, wrapping and LLM campaigns covering the 102-input corpus, and a recovery comparison on 49 previously problematic inputs. The evaluation distinguishes construction, functionality, detection, diversity, and resource use; it also reports an exploratory paired comparison of prompting conditions. The new methods and evaluation in Sections 8-12 occupy roughly 40% of the current manuscript by page extent, in addition to the expanded synthesis in Sections 13-14. This is an approximate space-based estimate; the substantive additions are documented individually rather than inferred from text similarity alone. The concluding future-work section identifies PE support as a way to broaden testing and assess practical applicability on complete applications, alongside a wider functional-validation programme.
